# CFP-FishMOT research evidence report

**IN_PROGRESS — not a final scientific conclusion.** Updated 2026-10-12. No independent method gain or SOTA claim exists yet.

Current evidence: migration/preflight checks pass; historical metric replay recovers HOTA 31.0911 / AssA 19.6192 / DetA 49.5748. Fifteen requested reference repositories were inspected at pinned commits, with partial-training and license restrictions documented. A six-video/train two-video/dev association protocol is frozen, with historical detector exposure disclosed. Thirty-three infrastructure/causality/diagnostic tests pass. Full cu117 detector inference reproduces all 15,985 frames' cache arrays exactly and all seven historical track files byte-for-byte, including the historical TrackEval results. The new appearance tracker's disabled path also reproduces all seven files exactly.

New full detector inference on the inherited cu118 runtime gives HOTA 30.907 / AssA 19.371 / DetA 49.580 / IDF1 34.684 / IDSW 568. Restoring cu117/cuDNN 8.5 resolves this drift on the full dataset. cu118/cu117 full elapsed times were 17m50.55s/18m12.98s, including decoding/tracking/evaluation; these are not strict-online latencies. Frozen shared ImageNet ResNet50 features were extracted for 14,528 paired GT crops in 40.02s. Exploratory legacy-input identity retrieval shows equal fusion improves BT-005 but harms SN-013. Three-seed RGB fish ReID is complete: development past-sample oracle retrieval is 29.153±1.285% on BT-005 and 50.212±1.485% on SN-013 (sample std), improving over frozen RGB. These are representation measures, not IDF1. Causal IR ReID and paired proposal features are running. Trained CMUE/PLIM, fair appearance tracker comparisons, three-seed tracking ablations and final frozen evaluation remain unperformed. Thus no answer yet exists for CMUE vs quality gate or PLIM vs EMA.

Research problem: conditional identity usefulness of constructed IR under limited representation/model capacity, plus causal latent identity prediction and separate reliable memory writes. Four recovered original generators are locally audited and their SHA256 hashes match NAS. Whole-sequence median backgrounds and adaptive strategy selection use future RGB frames, so historical full pipelines are not fully causal. A separate current/past-only EMA version is complete for 32,085 original train frames, taking 1,883.66s wall time with eight CPU workers; summed response-generation time is 11,921.92s, mean 0.3716s/frame under that load. Offline parallel wall time is not a single-stream online FPS benchmark. Cached geometry/component lookup preserve exact pixels on twelve sampled full-resolution frames; standalone steady-state generation is about 0.30–0.34s/frame. Prefix-state chunk scheduling is byte-equivalent on 128 generated images. Detector evaluation/NMS preserves prefix outputs exactly when later batch inputs change. All preprocessing must count in end-to-end costs. Capacity/current-contrast/matched-history controls are declared, with implementation/evaluation pending. Four ambiguous SN-013 boxes are excluded from identity samples and masked as official distractors in versioned derived GT; original data is preserved. Proposed contributions must survive fair controls and interventions.

The canonical causal development bank contains 4,834 frames / 51,286 proposals, V3 frozen, confidence 0.1/NMS 0.7, generated in 313.30s. Original ByteTrack gives HOTA 49.2967 / AssA 34.0115 / DetA 71.5087 / IDF1 58.8771 / MOTA 92.5292 / IDSW 86 / FP 1,353 / FN 2,032 / Frag 349 using audited GT. These development videos differ from historical test videos; these numbers are not a gain over historical HOTA 31.091. No appearance, CMUE or PLIM tracking improvement is established yet. Historical detector exposure remains disclosed; this is association development.

Actual artifacts are linked through the experiment registry and FAILURE_ANALYSIS.md. Final goal remains active. This document will be rewritten around completed evidence after the declared research phases, including failures and any unsupported hypotheses.
